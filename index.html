₦200M+ in Revenue in 10 Months for a D2C Food Brand

Overview

Over 10 months, I helped build a system that turned leads into paying customers across four acquisition and retention channels. This case study walks through my approach and the strategies that helped the brand close more than ₦200M in revenue.

The customer journey

The brand ran as a cloud kitchen, with no walk-ins and no pickups. Operations were simple: order request, order processing, delivery. Our acquisition channels needed to match that simplicity.

The two touchpoints that turned leads into buyers were the website and WhatsApp DMs. With no physical location, paid media was our most important channel for filling the funnel, and that's where the first problems started.

The challenge

The brand already had an SEO-optimised website and a familiar name, built first on its founders' personal networks. That wasn't a problem in itself, but it raised a big question: could the business thrive beyond that circle?

Three areas were holding it back: Meta Ads, email marketing and brand trust.

Meta Ads

  • Volume problem: more enquiries didn't translate into more orders.
  • Unprofitable costs that didn't lift daily order volume.
  • A campaign structure working against itself: fatigued creatives, fragmented ads, and no clear line between scaling to new customers and getting more value from existing ones.

Email marketing

  • Customers joined the list and stayed there, with no upsells and no effort to increase lifetime value.
  • Order confirmation, delivery status and follow-up emails weren't automated.

Brand trust

  • Buying food from a brand you can't see or visit is a hard sell. Many potential customers hesitated because they didn't know who was cooking their food or how it was made.

What I did

Meta Ads

  • Built a full-funnel structure, testing and optimising separate top, middle and bottom-of-funnel audiences.
  • Made the case for a bigger budget across all paid channels, not just Meta.
  • Consolidated campaigns and ad sets so the algorithm had room to learn.
  • Built a copy library for continuous testing, keeping creatives fresh and out of fatigue.

Result: Average CAC dropped by 60%, from ₦1,000 to ₦400, and quality leads rose by about 10% at launch.

Email marketing and cart recovery

  • Built a three-step recovery process for abandoned carts: an automated email, then a WhatsApp message, then a phone call. Because we handled around 10 abandoned carts a week, every one got a personal follow-up, and we recovered about 8 in 10 within 12 hours.
  • Automated order details and progress updates so customers always knew where their food was.
  • Built a re-engagement flow to win back lapsed customers and upsell them.

Result: Email drove more than 6% of monthly revenue.

Brand trust

I led a founder-fronted campaign that told the brand's story without a filter. We followed it with a trust campaign showing customers exactly how their meals are made from start to finish, built around two promises: "ingredients freshly sourced from the market" and "every meal prepared to your order."

Result: Run across all owned and paid channels, these campaigns doubled the brand's previous best daily revenue.

The bigger picture

Working with the team, I combined paid channels (Meta, Google, Snapchat, and later TikTok) with owned channels (Instagram, WhatsApp follow-ups, email, SMS and day-specific offers) into one acquisition and retention system.

When we introduced TikTok, it increased website sessions by more than 50% and lifted total orders by 30% in its first week.

Together, these efforts helped the brand close more than ₦200M in revenue within 10 months.
